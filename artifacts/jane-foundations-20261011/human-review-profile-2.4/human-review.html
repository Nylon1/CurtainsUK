Review procedure

Use two named people, including an interior-design reviewer. Each records independent scores and room-specific reasons before discussing disagreements. Review all three turns, not isolated replies. Separate personal taste from suitability for the stated brief. Record where an alternative changes undertone, contrast, scale or texture and why it might work. Check that a changed preference replaces earlier assumptions while fixed furnishings remain fixed.

No critical factual/privacy/eligibility failure. Every dimension >=3; second human review for ambiguous design advice. Compare blind-labelled real vs mock transcripts, then reveal provider. Record reasons and disagreements.

  • Understands the room and preserves constraints
  • Explains colour/undertone and pattern/texture relationships plausibly
  • Offers meaningfully different options
  • Adapts to feedback without defending earlier advice
  • Asks few relevant questions; concise natural British voice
  • Accurate current tool guidance
  • Evidence and eligibility discipline
  • Useful personalised summary and next steps

Critical failures

  • Invented manufacturer fact, stock, price, product or image
  • False exact colour guarantee or nonexistent tool feature
  • Leaked private information
  • Unconsented handover or claim that an unsaved operation succeeded

Write scores and evidence into the accompanying blank scorecards. No score is pre-filled. A failed or missing dimension cannot approve launch. Preserve reviewer identity, date and transcript version. The owner must nominate the reviewers; this document does not claim they have participated.

Revised real-model transcript

Customer

I want warm contemporary curtains. The room has oak flooring and cream walls.

Reply

With oak flooring and cream walls, I’d lean towards warm stone or oatmeal curtains with a softly textured, matt finish. That would echo the room’s warmth while keeping the look contemporary rather than rustic. For a more distinctive contrast, muted clay could add warmth and definition without a strong pattern. Would you prefer the curtains to blend quietly into the room or become a noticeable feature?

Customer

The room faces north. I want a warm feel without yellow or gold.

Reply

For your north-facing room, I’d shift towards a pale mushroom or soft putty with a gentle pink-brown undertone, rather than yellow-based oatmeal. Alongside the oak and cream, that could feel warm without looking golden. A plain, matt fabric with subtle woven texture would keep things contemporary. For more definition, muted terracotta offers a warmer contrast, though it would make the curtains more of a feature.

Customer

Summarise two different directions and why each might work.

Reply

1. Quiet and tonal: pale mushroom or soft putty with pink-brown undertones, in a plain, matt weave. This could soften the cooler north-facing light and sit gently alongside cream walls and oak flooring, without introducing yellow or gold. 2. Warm and contrasting: muted terracotta with a restrained texture and no bold pattern. Its earthy red undertone could bring warmth and give the windows more definition, but would be a stronger feature. For either direction, check a physical fabric swatch beside the walls and floor in daylight and evening lighting, as undertones can shift.

How does Fabric Intelligence help me choose curtains?

Reply

Fabric Intelligence helps turn what you want from your curtains, such as privacy, light control or a particular atmosphere, into five design directions with fabric suggestions and explanations of why they may suit. You can respond separately to the overall look and each fabric, then refine a shortlist. That means liking a colour direction needn’t mean liking every pattern. It’s a guided starting point, not a guarantee of the perfect fabric; physical samples remain important for judging colour and texture at home.

Customer

I dislike the recommendations. What should I change?

Reply

Change the part that feels wrong rather than starting over. If the results are too busy, revisit your pattern preference. If the colours miss the mark, revise your brief or correct the optional image palette. If you like the overall direction but dislike one fabric, keep that direction and give separate feedback on the fabric before refining the shortlist. Is it mainly the colours, the patterns or the overall atmosphere that doesn’t feel right?

Customer

Reply

Room Visualiser 2 doesn’t have a heading selector, so you can’t change it within the room preview. Open the separate Curtain Style page at /pages/fabric-library?view=curtain-style to compare Wave, Double Pinch Pleat, Pencil Pleat and Eyelet. These are reference images, not your chosen fabric in your room. For an actual order, the compatible heading is selected in Make Curtains after your window and track or pole choices.
