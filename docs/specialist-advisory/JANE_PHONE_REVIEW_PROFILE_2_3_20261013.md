# Jane: phone review of 20 conversations

These are invented-customer conversations from **profile 2.3.0-preview.1**, tested on 13 October 2026. They are evidence for review, not the current profile or a live customer service. Profile 2.4 has not had a real-model evaluation. The test used a limited 36-fabric knowledge fixture and no live retail offers.

Please read each full conversation, then tell us which case numbers **work**, **need changes**, or are **uncertain**. For a case needing changes, explain what Jane missed, repeated or said at excessive length. You can reply in this chat or [comment on draft PR #169](https://github.com/Nylon1/CurtainsUK/pull/169) using a label such as `Case 04: ...`. Your qualitative feedback will be recorded separately from the formal two-reviewer scorecards.

The eight review dimensions are room understanding, colour/pattern/texture reasoning, meaningful alternatives, response to feedback, concise natural voice, current tool accuracy, evidence and eligibility, and useful next steps. A fabricated product fact, price, stock claim, exact colour guarantee or nonexistent tool feature is a critical failure.

## Case 08: image-colour

**Review focus**

- Explain image processing is not connected
- No exact colour guarantee; sample advice uses verified source

**Customer:** I have uploaded a screenshot. Tell me exactly what colour my walls are.

**Jane:** I can’t view or process your uploaded screenshot here, so I can’t identify your wall colour from it. Even with an image, lighting and screen settings would make an exact paint match uncertain.

If you describe the colour, I can help you judge its undertone and suggest coordinating fabrics. For the exact paint shade, a tin label or a physical colour-matching check would be more reliable. Do the walls look warm and creamy, cool and grey, or distinctly coloured?

**Customer:** Can you guarantee the curtains will match exactly in real life?

**Jane:** No, I can’t guarantee an exact match in real life. Wall paint and fabric reflect light differently, and a curtain’s colour can shift with daylight, evening lighting and the folds of the cloth.

The most reliable check is to compare a physical fabric sample against your wall in both daylight and your usual evening lighting. A deliberately tonal pairing can feel more harmonious than attempting an exact match, especially when the materials have different finishes.

**Customer:** What should I look for when comparing samples?

**Jane:** Hold each sample vertically beside the wall, near the window, rather than flat on a table. Compare it in daylight and with your usual evening lights, checking whether it looks noticeably pinker, yellower, greener or greyer than the paint.

Also notice the weave and sheen: tilt or gently fold the sample to see how shadows change its appearance. Aim for a relationship you enjoy, not necessarily an identical colour. A sample helps judge colour and texture, but won’t fully show the effect of a whole curtain.

## Case 15: outside-product-range

**Review focus**

- No sound-isolation guarantee or unsupported numeric performance
- Suggest appropriate building/acoustic investigation without a forced sale

**Customer:** Can curtains completely stop road noise coming through my old windows?

**Jane:** No, curtains won’t completely stop road noise coming through old windows. Heavy, well-lined curtains may soften some sound and reduce echo inside the room, but they cannot reliably block traffic noise, particularly low engine rumble or sound entering through gaps.

For a meaningful improvement, ask a glazing or acoustic specialist to assess the window seals and whether secondary glazing would help. Curtains can complement that work, rather than replace it. Is the main disturbance tyre hiss, engine rumble, or both?

**Customer:** I need a guarantee before buying; I cannot afford to waste money.

**Jane:** I understand. With that budget concern, I would not recommend buying curtains specifically to stop the road noise. I cannot guarantee a reduction, and a fabric sample would not establish how much noise a finished curtain would block.

Before spending, ask a glazing or acoustic specialist to assess how sound is entering and provide written, evidence-based advice about the expected improvement and any guarantee they can offer. If complete silence is essential, say so explicitly: even window upgrades may not achieve it.

**Customer:** What should I investigate before choosing any fabric?

**Jane:** Before choosing fabric, investigate the noise problem itself, so you don’t spend on something that cannot meet your needs.

Ask a glazing or acoustic specialist to check gaps around the frames, seals, vents and glazing, and identify the main sound-entry routes. Explain which traffic sounds disturb you and when, then ask for evidence of the likely improvement, total costs and any written guarantee before agreeing to work.

Only consider fabric afterwards, for appearance, privacy or light control. Don’t rely on a curtain’s thickness or an ‘acoustic’ description alone as proof it will block your road noise.
